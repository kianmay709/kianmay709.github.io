01 / Executive summary

The operational picture

Northline Home Co. is a fictional UK home accessories business selling primarily through Shopify, with a small secondary marketplace channel. This example uses invented but internally consistent data to demonstrate how a Stonevale audit could identify operational risks and set priorities.

Executive finding.

Late dispatches cluster around manual stock checks and inconsistent pick/pack handoffs. Inventory exceptions and return reasons should be monitored in one weekly scorecard before any automation is introduced.

Three priority themes

Priority 01 · fulfilment

Dispatch reliability

114 orders missed the internal dispatch target. The example dataset records 42 orders with pick/pack exceptions, though the overlap with late orders is not established.

Priority 02 · inventory

Stock visibility

Nine of 48 active SKUs experienced at least one stockout during the review. The underlying record does not establish total lost sales, so no revenue-recovery figure is claimed.

Priority 03 · reporting

Manual checks

Two operations staff estimate a combined 12 hours per week preparing reconciliations, exception lists and reports. This is self-reported time, not independently timed activity.

03 / Fulfilment & inventory

Where the friction appears

Dispatch performance

Of 1,200 orders, 1,086 were dispatched by the internal target and 114 were late.

On time90.5%
Late9.5%

The 42 pick/pack exceptions represent 3.5% of orders; they should not be added to late dispatches without checking overlap.

Inventory observations

48 active SKUs were reviewed. Nine experienced one or more stockouts in the 60-day window.

SKUs with stockout events9 / 48

This is 18.75% of active SKUs, not 18.75% of orders or 18.75% revenue lost.

Working hypotheses to test

Start with an exception log and weekly fulfilment scorecard. Only then decide whether inventory alerts and dispatch handoff automations would address verified causes.

04 / Returns & workflow reporting

Two areas to investigate

Returns and refunds

The sample records 78 refunded orders with a combined £3,900 of refunds. That is 6.5% of orders with refunds and a refund-value ratio of 6.25% of gross order value. These different measures should not be confused.

Reason recorded for refunded ordersOrders
Damaged in transit28
Product not as expected24
Wrong item / picking issue14
Other or unspecified12
Total78

These labels are based on fictional refund records and are not proof that a seller action caused each refund. They suggest a need to sample supporting tickets, packaging evidence and product descriptions.

Reporting and manual administration

The example team reports spending approximately 12 staff-hours each week reconciling orders, checking stock exceptions and preparing management summaries.

Automation candidate.

Create a central order-exceptions table and a daily exception notification. Build a prototype on a small data sample, test false alerts and assign responsibility for maintenance. No time-saving claim should be made before measuring the new process.

06 / Suggested 30-day sequence

From diagnosis to a measurable process

Week 1

Verify dispatch definition, open exceptions log, establish baseline.

Week 2

Review stockout SKUs and sample 30 refund records.

Week 3

Introduce scorecard and agree owners for recurring exceptions.

Week 4

Pilot one notification workflow; compare against baseline.

No guaranteed financial return.

The sample does not convert problems into claimed recoverable profit. To estimate financial impact responsibly, we'd need item margins, actual cause attribution, expected intervention costs, order mix and post-change measurements.

What a real engagement would include

Agreed scope, controlled data access, documented metric definitions, evidence-backed findings, prioritised recommendations and a results discussion. Implementation or ongoing support is separately scoped.
